Broadcom's AI Chip Business Grew 221% in One Quarter. Almost None of Those Chips Carry Broadcom's Name.

Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Anthropic, and OpenAI are all building their own AI chips to cut loose from Nvidia. Almost none of them do the hard engineering alone: an estimated ~95% of that design work funnels through just two outside firms, Broadcom and Marvell. Broadcom just told investors its biggest customer in 2027 won't even be Google — it'll be Anthropic.

Key takeaway In its fiscal Q3 2026 results (reported Sept 2026), Broadcom said AI semiconductor revenue hit $16.7 billion for the quarter, up 221% year over year and 56% of total revenue, with Q4 guided to $21.7 billion. CEO Hock Tan told analysts Anthropic "is on track to become our largest XPU customer in 2027 and sustain that in 2028," with Anthropic deploying 5 gigawatts of custom TPU v8i capacity in 2027 and 10 more in 2028. Tom's Hardware's analysis puts Broadcom and Marvell's combined share of the hyperscale custom-AI-chip co-design market at roughly 95%; Counterpoint Research separately projects Broadcom's own share at about 60% by 2027, with Marvell slipping to 8%, and estimates TSMC fabricates roughly 99% of the wafers for the top 10 AI-server-ASIC shippers.

The plain version

Picture six of the biggest tech companies deciding they're tired of buying the same off-the-shelf AI chip from Nvidia, so each one hires an architect to design them a custom house instead — a chip built exactly for their own AI workloads. That's the story you usually hear: Google has its TPU, Amazon has Trainium, Meta has MTIA, Microsoft has Maia, and now Anthropic and OpenAI are getting their own custom chips too.

Here's the part that doesn't make the headlines: almost none of these companies actually know how to turn a rough sketch of a chip into something a factory can build. That's an extremely specialized skill, and nearly every one of them hires the same couple of outside firms to do it — Broadcom and Marvell. By one analysis, those two firms handle roughly 95% of this "co-design" work combined, across virtually every hyperscaler's program.

So the industry's big "escape from Nvidia" story is real in one sense — these companies really are buying fewer general-purpose Nvidia GPUs. But the actual engineering bottleneck didn't disappear; it just moved one step down the chain, into a market that's just as concentrated. Broadcom just told Wall Street its AI chip-design business grew 221% in a single quarter, and that its single biggest customer next year won't be its longtime anchor client Google — it'll be Anthropic, a company that had no custom chip at all a couple of years ago.

Why it matters for tech + supply chain: if you're counting on hyperscalers' custom chips to break Nvidia's pricing power, the leverage didn't vanish — it landed on two companies most people have never heard of.

The expert version

A custom AI accelerator (an ASIC, or application-specific integrated circuit, as opposed to a merchant GPU) still has to go through physical design, SerDes and high-speed interconnect IP integration, HBM memory-controller integration, verification, and tape-out before a foundry can build it. Hyperscalers typically define the architecture and workload-specific requirements, but the deep, leading-edge physical-design and packaging-integration expertise required to execute that spec is concentrated in a small number of design-services firms. Tom's Hardware's analysis of the custom-AI-ASIC landscape puts Broadcom's and Marvell's combined share of this co-design market at roughly 95%; other estimates (Hashrate Index, citing Bloomberg Intelligence) put Broadcom alone around 70% and Marvell around 20–25%, while Counterpoint Research's January 2026 forecast projects Broadcom at about 60% and Marvell falling to about 8% of design-service share by 2027, with Alchip near 18%. The spread reflects differing methodologies (unit share vs. dollar share vs. design-win count), but every estimate agrees the market is a near-duopoly.

Broadcom's own disclosures quantify how fast this is scaling. Fiscal Q3 2026 AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7 billion, up 221% year over year and 56% of total company revenue ($29.6 billion, up 86% YoY); Q4 guidance calls for $21.7 billion in AI semiconductor revenue (up 236% YoY), implying roughly $58 billion for fiscal 2026 overall. Management's multi-year guidance has AI semiconductor revenue roughly doubling to ~$115 billion in fiscal 2027 and doubling again to ~$230 billion in fiscal 2028. CEO Hock Tan told analysts Anthropic is on track to overtake Google as Broadcom's largest XPU (custom accelerator) customer in 2027 and hold that position in 2028, with Anthropic committing to 5 gigawatts of custom TPU v8i capacity in 2027 and an incremental 10 gigawatts in 2028. Separately, Marvell has disclosed roughly $1.5 billion in fiscal 2026 custom XPU revenue (principally tied to AWS Trainium), guided to grow at least 20% in fiscal 2027 and double again in fiscal 2028.

Crucially, diversifying the design layer doesn't diversify the foundry layer: Counterpoint estimates TSMC fabricates roughly 99% of the wafers for the top 10 companies shipping AI server ASICs — frequently on the same leading-edge nodes Nvidia itself uses, and feeding into the same CoWoS-class advanced-packaging capacity covered in this newsletter's November 11 edition on TSMC's packaging price hikes.

Why it matters for tech + supply chain: "custom silicon" reduces hyperscalers' dependence on Nvidia's margins, but it does not disperse supply-chain risk — it concentrates a new, structurally similar chokepoint around two design-services firms and one foundry.
