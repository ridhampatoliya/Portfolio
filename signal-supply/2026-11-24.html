November 24, 2026 COPPER MARKETS

Copper's AI Boom Erased 16% of Its Value in Ten Weeks. The Physical Metal Got Scarcer the Whole Time.

In September, traders bid copper to an all-time high on the theory that AI data centers would need almost unlimited amounts of wiring. By mid-November that bet had unwound — the price fell 16%. But the premium Chinese buyers pay to actually get their hands on physical copper hit a record the very same week.

Key takeaway The copper price crashed and the copper shortage got worse, in the same two weeks. That's not a contradiction — it's what happens when the market that prices copper (traders betting on the future) and the market that moves copper (factories that need it this month) stop agreeing with each other.

Drag through Sept–Nov 2026. Watch the paper price and the physical premium split apart.

The plain version

Strip away the GPUs, and an AI data center is, structurally, an enormous amount of copper — busbars, power rails, cooling loops, grid hookups, the whole building wired twice over because redundancy is the point. Traders spent much of 2026 betting that the AI boom alone would drain the world's copper supply, and in September that bet paid off spectacularly: the price of copper on the London Metal Exchange hit an all-time high of $14,875 a ton.

Then it fell apart. By November 18, that same copper had dropped 16%, to $12,471 a ton, as investors decided AI's appetite for metal wasn't showing up on the schedule they'd priced in, and started unwinding the bet. Here's the strange part: while the price of a copper contract was crashing, the price of actually getting your hands on copper was doing the opposite. In China, the extra amount buyers pay above the benchmark price just to secure real, physical metal — not a futures contract, actual rolls of wire sitting in a warehouse — jumped to a record $360 a ton, more than four times what it cost in September.

Think of it like concert tickets: the official face-value price can crash because speculators stopped bidding it up, even while the people outside the venue who need a seat tonight are paying more than ever, because there still aren't enough seats. The paper market and the physical market just told two opposite stories about the same metal, in the same two weeks — and copper is wired into literally everything the AI boom is building.

The expert version

Two separate markets price the same ton of copper, and in November 2026 they diverged hard. The LME 3-month futures contract — the "paper" benchmark that sets the global reference price — ran from an all-time high of $14,875/ton on September 10 (driven by AI-data-center demand bets and tariff-driven stockpiling) down to a $12,471/ton close on November 18, a roughly 16% retreat to a four-month low, as speculative long positions unwound on fading conviction that AI buildouts would absorb the metal on the timeline the rally had priced in.

Simultaneously, the Yangshan copper premium — the spot surcharge Chinese importers pay above the LME price to secure physical delivery into bonded warehouses near Shanghai — rose from under $100/ton in September to $350 on November 17 and $360 on November 18, roughly a 4x increase. Shanghai Futures Exchange warehouse inventories fell by an estimated 18% in a single week to their lowest level in nearly three years over the same window. Paper price and physical premium are supposed to move together; a divergence this sharp is the textbook signature of speculative positioning (driving the futures price) decoupling from physical tightness (driving the premium).

The supply picture underneath is genuinely unsettled, not just volatile sentiment. The International Copper Study Group forecast a 150,000-ton global deficit for 2026 as of October 2025, flipped to a roughly 96,000-ton surplus forecast by April 2026, then reported an actual 51,000-ton deficit in July — three contradictory readings inside one year. Chile's Codelco, the world's largest copper miner, shut its Andina concentrator on November 5 after a tailings spill reached the Blanco River, removing supply with no resolution date yet. And estimates of how much copper an AI data center actually needs span a wide range — roughly 27 to 50 tonnes per megawatt of capacity, per separate S&P Global and Kpler analyses — a nearly 2x spread that underscores how much of the "AI needs copper" thesis still rests on modeling assumptions rather than settled measurement.

Why it matters for tech + supply chain: Copper is the literal wiring of the AI boom, so a 16% price swing driven by sentiment, running opposite to physical scarcity, is an early, measurable crack in how fragile AI-infrastructure pricing really is.

Why it matters for tech + supply chain: When a benchmark futures price and the physical premium that's supposed to track it move in opposite directions this sharply, it signals the "AI demand" narrative priced into industrial commodities is running ahead of — or behind — what's actually moving through ports and warehouses, and either gap eventually has to close.
